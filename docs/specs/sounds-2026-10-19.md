# Sounds from the owner's playtest notes, and the sound studio (v2.3.3521)

The owner's notes after the 2026-10-19 playtest, the sound part:

> Fix duck squeak sound effect for bow shot (should be current "jump" sound).
> Wrong sound for starting sprint and ending sprint (hurt sound) instead of
> effort grunt. I think sounds were wired in the wrong places. The labels
> should tell you where they go. Might need to build a quick studio to show
> which sounds belong where. The sound when a tree hits 0 hp is a fleshy
> sound. Remove that. [...] Change weapon switch sound. Breaking the ore sound
> doesn't sound right anymore. Old sound was better. [...] Hit made a fleshy
> sound on the snowman (maybe crit?) can't be that sound. Same fleshy sound
> for the rock monster on every hit. Same with hitting crystals probably
> other props too. Find different sound for the points leveling up sound
> (maybe just speed the existing sound up?)

Every change below was **measured** before it was made, by rendering exactly
what plays (offset, length, rate, gain and fade applied): the share of the
energy under 150, 250 and 400 Hz, where a body's thump lives, and the
loudness of the loudest 50 ms against the game's own hit (sword-hit3 at
0.55 x HIT_GAIN, "1.0"). The game's "fleshy" sample, `monster-hit`, is 98%
under 250 Hz.

Two notes from the same list are in other PRs: the trap's sounds in the
combat-feel PR (#1025, `trapSet`/`trapShake`/`trapSnap`/`trapMiss`), and the
forging sword's strike, which was the pickaxe on rock, in the Blacksmith PR
(#1027).

## 1. The bow's shot is the jump's rush of air

The "duck squeak" was the cue `bowDraw`: bow-pullback at 3.6x and, as the
shot played it, 1.3x the rate. That is a creak whose peaks sat at 1.9-2.5
kHz, 0.64 of a hit, five times the arrow's own flight under it (0.12), so it
was nearly all you heard.

- `bowDraw` is gone. `bowLoose` (data/eventSounds.js) is the slice the jump's
  push-off was: the arrow's own flight, quick, ~0.4, at the moment the arrow
  leaves (0.11 s, the release frame).
- It plays for your shot (game/monsterCombat.js), for each arrow of the bow's
  volley on its own launch wait (playerActions.js), and for another player's
  shot at their share (gameEvents.js). The last two go through
  `cueLater(name, extra, k)` (game/eventSounds.js), which moves every layer
  `extra` seconds later.
- The jump keeps the same rush, a touch lower and slower (rate 0.85, 0.22 s),
  so the two can be told apart.

## 2. The sprint starts and ends with the effort grunt

The owner sent two grunts, and they were wired the wrong way round for how
they **sound**:

- "Grunt 1" (85280) starts at ~330 Hz, falls to ~150 Hz, and is about 900 Hz
  bright. That is the shape and colour of the hurt "uh!" (male_hurt7 48124,
  320 -> 143 Hz, ~910 Hz). It was the sprint's.
- "Angry Grunt" (103204) is a low strained "hnh" at 80-90 Hz, ~400 Hz bright:
  the effort. It was the second hurt sound.

So:

- `public/sfx/voice/sprint-grunt.mp3` is cut from the angry grunt by
  `tools/audio/cut_sprint_voice.py` (0.22 s).
  - It is high-passed at 220 Hz, which takes its 80-90 Hz rumble off and
    keeps its strain whole: the harmonics a phone's speaker actually plays.
  - It is levelled **by ear** (A-weighted), at the old grunt's own level by
    ear (`GRUNT_EAR_K` 0.667 of a hit). On the plain scale it came out 3.6 dB
    quieter to the ear than the grunt it replaces.
- `public/sfx/player/hurt-2.mp3` is cut from Grunt 1 by
  `tools/audio/cut_owner_sounds.py`.
- `BT_AUDIO.SPRINT_PUSH` (a sprint's start) and the cue `sprintOut` (a sprint
  running dry, the grunt a touch lower and slower) read the new file. The
  tired breath every other time is unchanged.

The worn-out grunt measures ~0.45 of a hit on the plain scale, which weighs
its 200-400 Hz strain whole, and ~0.3 by ear. Most of its energy is under
400 Hz, as a voiced grunt's is. So mp-cuelevels' rush-of-air check (the
"fleshy" push-off, v2.3.3336) no longer tells it from a thump.

It moved to a check of its own, 6. **a grunt is a voice, not a thump**:
`sprintOut` and the two hurt grunts each put at most 0.6 of the old push-off's
share of their energy under 150 Hz, where a thump lives. CREDITS.md says which
recording is which.

**And a sprint begun mid-walk now pushes off** (found by mp-sprintpeer on
the way). `updateSprint` (game/sprint.js) reports a sprint's first stride as
`'run'`, the cue for the push-off's grunt and dust. When the sprint STARTED
on that same frame (Shift pressed while already walking, on a computer), it
kept the frame's `'start'` instead, which nothing plays. So that sprint had no
grunt and no dust at all. Now `'run'` wins (it says the sprint started too).
The sprint suite checks both orders.

## 3. A tree you chop down cracks and creaks, with no thump

It was `tree-fall` whole: 2 s at 0.8, a crack, a creak, then from 1.0 s a
thump 88-91% under 150 Hz, louder than anything in a fight (3x a hit).

- The cue `treeFelled` is now the axe's own dry crack plus tree-fall's first
  second alone, its crack and creak faded before the thump. It measures ~1.4,
  so a tree coming down is still the loudest thing about a harvest.
- It plays at the woodcutting payout (game/lifeSkillRewards.js).
- A Wheel tree broken by weapons (`PROP_SOUNDS.wood.tree`) plays the same.

## 4. A weapon swap sounds like the weapon

It was the old swing whoosh, fast (0.46 of a hit), and it read as an attack.
Now there are three cues, each ~0.4: the hand taking the weapon (the equip
tick's leather, soft) and the weapon itself.

- `swapSword`: the blade's ring.
- `swapBow`: a knock of its wood.
- `swapStaff`: a shimmer.

Each is picked by the slot switched to, for the weapon cycle and for a
computer's 1-3 keys (BroTown.jsx).

## 5. The ore cracks when the rock bursts

The sound never changed (`ore-crack`, v2.3.3040). Its **moment** did.

- The old strip split on its frame 7, `ORE_SPLIT_MS` in, and the crack played
  on the split.
- Since v2.3.3345 the rock bursts into its pieces the moment the break
  begins, and they have nearly landed by 350 ms. So the crack came after the
  burst: a lone crack under falling rubble.

Now (effectsRenderer `_advanceOreBreaks`):

- It cracks on the break's first frame, with the burst (`_oreCrack`).
- As the pieces land, with the ore's pop (`_oreSplit`), the stone break
  settles them: a broken rock prop's own sound, quieter.
- Another player's vein is softer and fades with distance, as before
  (`_oreBreakVol`).

## 6. No thump under the rock monster, the snowman or the objects

| What | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Your hit on a snowman (`HIT_VOICES.snow`) | snowman-hit, the snowball's thud, 91% under 250 Hz | a crunch of snow as the body, an icy crack over it (~0.7 of a hit, 19% under 250 Hz) |
| Your hit on a rock monster or thorn shambler (`HIT_VOICES.stone`) | the pick from 0.06 s (its click cut off: a dull body) over a footstep's thump as loud as the pick | the pick WITH its click (from 0.58 s, the town anvil's strike), two pitches, over a scrape of gravel |
| A big wooden thing (`PROP_SOUNDS.wood.big`: trees, gates, buildings) | wood-chop, 90% under 250 Hz | the axe's own dry knock |
| Stone, coal, ice and crystal objects | the pick from 0.06 s, dull | the pick with its click |
| A crystal | a ring over the dull pick | a ring over an icy crack |

The snow and gravel are the Wheel's footstep clips. Outside the Wheel each
voice plays its `fb`, which is what it made before. Flesh, goo, ember and wet
monsters keep their thud: the owner asked for it on slimes and goblins in
v2.3.2452.

## 7. Points spent: the same chime, quicker

The file is a slow sweep from ~120 to 460 Hz and a ring to 1.5 s, most of the
sweep under what a phone's speaker plays. At 1.8x (the owner's own
suggestion) it is a bright 0.56 s chime, its ring near 830 Hz, faded off its
tail (`pointsSpent`).

## 8. The sound studio

> I think sounds were wired in the wrong places. The labels should tell you
> where they go. Might need to build a quick studio to show which sounds
> belong where.

**Where:** Test panel (hold the zone's name) -> **Sound studio** -> "Open the
sound studio". Or add `?sounds` to the address, and it opens once you are in
the world.

- **Every sound in the game, under the moment it plays at, in plain words**
  ("Your bow shot", "A snowman throwing a snowball", "Hitting a crystal or
  obsidian"). It lists 156 cues plus everything played through BT_AUDIO's own
  tables:
  - your hits by what they land on
  - hitting and breaking objects, by material
  - a monster's ball breaking
  - the statuses on you
  - the monster noises
  - every ground's footstep
  - the sprint, the swim, deaths, the menu ticks, and the harvests' own
    sounds.

  They are grouped in 13 sections (fighting, your hits, getting hurt, moving,
  monsters, the world, gathering and the farm, crafting, loot and trading,
  other players, chat and emotes, pets, you), with a search box.
- **▶ plays it through the game's own player** (`cue`, `BT_AUDIO`), so it is
  exactly what the game plays where you stand. The ▶ makes no menu click, so
  the click never lands on the sound being heard.
- **The recordings each sound is made of**, as chips. Tap one to see every
  place that recording plays. A recording in two places that should not share
  it is "wired in the wrong place". The owner's own recordings say what they
  were when they came ("Angry Grunt" (freesound_community 103204)).
- **Heard just now:** the last sounds the game played, newest first, each by
  the moment it belongs to. Hear something wrong, open the studio, and it is
  at the top with its label. Menu clicks are left out unless asked.

How:

- `src/data/soundStudio.js` is the catalogue: plain data, every label.
- `src/game/soundStudio.js` is always loaded and small. It remembers the
  last 48 sounds started (a ring written in place) by wrapping `BT_AUDIO.play`
  and each table helper. Each helper says what it is while its samples start
  (`BT_AUDIO._tag`; `cue`, `cueLater` and `peerOwn` say their cue's name). It
  also opens the studio.
- `src/ui/panels/SoundStudio.jsx` is the screen. It is loaded only when
  opened (its own 27 KB chunk) and taken off the page when closed. It reads
  no game state and writes none.

**Labels are kept true by the sound suite:** server/test/sound-cues.test.mjs
§12 fails when one of these has no "where it plays" line:

- a cue
- a key of HIT_VOICES, PROP_SOUNDS, SHOT_SOUNDS or ELEM_SOUNDS
- a monster noise
- a ground's footstep

It also fails for a line naming a sound that is gone, and for a row playing
a recording or helper that does not exist. A new sound is labelled in the
same change.
