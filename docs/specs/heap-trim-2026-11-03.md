2026-11-02/03 main's heap at mp-membudget's third-trip fight read about 36.3
MB, over its 36 MB line (#1391 raised it to 38). This lane measures what grew
it, trims it without changing what players see or do, and lowers the line.

## What the snapshots say (base vs main, unminified builds)

Two runs of the check's trip with a full heap snapshot (after two forced
collections) on arrival and at the first and third fights, on builds made
with `vite build --minify false` (so the functions keep their names): base
0be55a6f and main 9d5cc37b. Readings on those runs: arrival 31.5 -> 31.9 MB,
third fight 35.1 (0 swings) -> 36.4 (8 swings). Compared object kind by kind
(the page's own JavaScript only, not the browser's native objects):

- **On arrival, +0.42 MB**, spread thin: compiled code +137 KB (functions that
  are new and run on arrival: the quest card 12 KB, the place chip 7, the
  weapon triangle with its views ~16, the town board on the noticeboard 7,
  the guide arrow's state 3, the player frame 2, the quest offers 3, ...),
  strings +68 KB (the armour glow's four bigger shader sources ~53 KB, the
  quest journal's and townsfolk's new words), arrays and objects +150 KB (no
  single holder over 30 KB).
- **At the third fight, +1.7 MB, of which +1.2 MB is compiled code** (machine
  code +406 KB, bytecode +305 KB, their tables): what the engine compiles
  depends on how much of the game ran (8 swings against 0 on that trip), so
  most of it is the fight, not the merges. Everything that is not code grew
  ~100 KB.
- **No merge holds a big table or a leak.** The growth is the batch's code
  as it runs: dozens of new functions of 1-12 KB each, all of them used on
  the trip (the HUD's new parts: the quest card, the place chip, the weapon
  triangle, the player frame, the guide arrow, the plates' element icons, the
  death zones). Making them load later would change what players see, and
  the windows the trip never opens (the quest journal) hold little until run.

### So the trim comes from the biggest holders, wherever they are

Looking at the whole heap at the fight instead of the difference:

1. **The Wheel minimap's roads, river and railway: ~1.0 MB.** One Graphics
   (`wheelMinimap.js` `lines`) stroked once for the whole long Wheel, ~26,600
   points with their round joins. Pixi 8 keeps the three plain arrays it
   built the triangles from (`geometryData`: points 426 KB, texture places
   397 KB, corners 213 KB) for the session, although a drawing that big is
   never batched and is drawn from its own buffers, made once. The biggest
   single holder of the page's objects; the same in base.
2. **The old minimap's glyphs: ~0.3 MB.** `minimapRenderer.js` photographs
   each of its 18 glyphs and its dot into a texture and never destroyed the
   Graphics it drew them with (each kept its shapes and triangles: 17 such
   Graphics, 296 KB of lists). `_mintPin` already let its own go.

## The trims (commit "Heap trim: the minimap's road lists and baked glyphs let go")

Neither changes a pixel or a rule; both are what players never see.

1. **`src/rendering/gfxLean.js`, `leanGraphics(g)`**, called each frame from
   `wheelMinimap.js` `update()` for its `lines`, `buildings` and `places`
   (a few property reads; a no-op once done). Once Pixi has made a big,
   unbatched Graphics' own vertex and index buffers (`graphicsData`), the
   three lists it built them from (`geometryData`) are emptied as Pixi's own
   `reset()` empties them (`length = 0`). Pixi reads them again only when the
   drawing changes (it refills them itself) or after its collector unloads
   the context (a fresh build from the shapes). Left alone: a batched
   Graphics (under 400 numbers: Pixi copies those into the shared batch on
   every rebuild of its layer), one not drawn yet, a dirty one, one under
   4,000 numbers. Switch: `?gfxlean=0`, and the Test panel's Memory savers
   row "Big drawings kept once" (`clientOff.js`, from the next load; the
   probe `window.__btGfxLean`).
2. **`minimapRenderer.js` `_mintIcon` / `_makeDotTexture`**: the Graphics a
   glyph is drawn with is destroyed once `generateTexture` has photographed
   it (as `_mintPin` already did). The texture is the glyph; the Graphics
   was never drawn or read again, but each kept its shapes and Pixi's
   triangles, held by Pixi's own lists of drawables.

## The line

`heap` 38 -> **36** (tools/qa/mp/memory-budget.mjs, its header note
v2.3.4080). The trim's highest readings on this box were 34.2 and 34.5:
36 leaves ~1.5 MB of room here. CI's runner has read main ~0.7-1 MB above
this box (36.3 on two PRs at 9d5cc37b, where this box read 35.0-35.6), so
on CI the room is nearer 0.7-1 MB: the old line's room. The lanes about to
land (the Gear tab, the dashboard buttons, harder monsters, rare fish, the
climbing island) will spend some of it; each says so in its PR if it must
raise it.

## Decisions I made

- **Trimmed older code, not the batch's.** The batch's growth is its new
  code running on the trip, spread over dozens of functions each 1-12 KB,
  all of it visible HUD (the quest card, the place chip, the weapon
  triangle, the player frame, the plates' icons) or the fight (the death
  zones): nothing to load later without changing what players see. The two
  biggest wasted holders on the trip were older (the minimap's lists and
  glyphs) and won back more than the batch added.
- **Not made lazy: the quest journal** (QuestsPanel, QuestDetailPanel,
  QuestJournalPage, questJournalModel). Never opened on the trip, its code
  costs little until run (a function's machine code is made when it runs),
  and its imports sit in BottomDashboard.jsx, which lane B is reshaping. A
  later lane can move it behind lazyPanels.js for a few tens of KB.
- **The switch is a Test-panel row too** (`gfxlean`, from the next load),
  as every memory saver since v2.3.3755, so the owner can turn it off for
  every phone without a deploy.
- **The line is 36, an absolute number**, chosen against this box's
  readings plus ~1.5 MB; CI's room will be smaller (see above). If CI's
  first runs on main read above ~35, 36.5 is the next step, with a note.
