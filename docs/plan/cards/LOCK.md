## The readings, chosen so you do not guess

1. **Measure first, in node.** A census (kept as a tool, `tools/qa/lock-census.mjs`, its numbers in the PR body): over a few thousand made-up fights (two to eight monsters walking and closing, the player walking, the ring as in the game), how often and for how long the lock is NOT on the nearest, split by cause: the 12% step; the held lock's 550 against the ring's 440; a distance measured to a point of the monster that is not where it looks to be (its feet against its picture, a tall or wide monster: say what the code measures to); anything else you find. This says which mend matters; the mends below are done anyway unless the census shows one is harmful.
2. **Near means near.** Inside the sword's own reach (the reach the hit test uses, plus the lunge's if the lock feeds it: name the number you chose and why), the nearest ALWAYS has the lock: no 12% step. The flip-flop the step was made for is answered there by TIME instead: a rival must have been nearer for a short dwell (start at 120 ms; a number in the table) before it takes the lock. Outside reach the 12% step stays.
3. **The stick chooses between close ones.** When the two nearest are within 25% of each other (a number in the table) and the stick is pushed past its dead zone, the one nearer the PUSH's direction takes the lock (no dwell: the player asked). With the stick at rest, nothing changes by facing. This is "let you switch": pushing toward a monster in a pack picks it. It never picks a monster outside the ring, and never one more than 25% farther than the nearest.
4. **What a far lock does when a near one arrives**: a held lock between 440 and 550 gives way at once to ANY candidate inside reach (reading 2 covers it; prove it).
5. **One rule, one module.** The pick is a pure function in `src/game/` (`lockPick.js`: candidates, the held lock, the stick's push, the clock -> the pick and why), called by `updateTargeting`; `targeting.js` loses those lines. The numbers live in ONE frozen table there.
6. **A way back**: `?locknear=0` gives today's rule whole on one phone (ON by default: the owner is the only player); its row in the switch table (`tools/guards/lib/switches.mjs`, `--write`).
7. **Not this card's**: tapping a monster to lock it (the owner's own earlier no; the orchestrator asks them), the bow and the staff, mode 5, the server's `targetId` checks, how the lock is drawn.
