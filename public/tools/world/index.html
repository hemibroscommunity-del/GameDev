How this works

  1. The grid. Squares are named like a spreadsheet. Today's world is the 13 × 13 block from G7 to S19, with M13 — the town square — in the middle. It sits inside a bigger 25 × 25 frame so the world can grow later without renaming or moving a single square. Each square is one ChatGPT picture, and each overlaps its neighbours by a quarter so there is room to hide the join.
  2. The plan. The flat colours are the blueprint: Brotown's Main Street and its empty building plots, the Old Roads to every region, the Sweetwater River and its bridge, the mine railway, the eight regions round the Starting Meadow, ponds, cliffs and landmarks. It keeps roads and rivers continuous across squares — and later it becomes the game's walls.
  3. The style key. One picture of the map's look, made before the first square and attached to every chat, so every square matches it.
  4. The template. Each square's template shows its piece of the plan with the finished art of its neighbours already in place along the edges, so ChatGPT continues real pixels instead of guessing.
  5. The fuse. ChatGPT never copies the edges exactly. The page lines its picture up, matches the colour, and cuts the seam where the old and new pictures agree most — round a tree rather than through it.
  6. Order. Style key first, then M13, then outward: the gold-outlined squares are ready because their neighbours are finished. If the plan ever changes under a painted square, the page names it.
