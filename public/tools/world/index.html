How this works

  1. The grid. The world is a 12 × 12 grid of squares named like a spreadsheet — A1 is the north-west corner. Each square is one ChatGPT picture, and each overlaps its neighbours by a quarter so there is room to hide the join.
  2. The plan. The flat colours are the blueprint: the eight regions round the town where the World View painting has them, the meadow ring, trails from the town's stairs, ponds, cliffs and landmarks. It keeps rivers and trails continuous across squares — and later it becomes the game's walls.
  3. The template. Each square's template shows its piece of the plan with the finished art of its neighbours (and the town) already in place along the edges, so ChatGPT continues real pixels instead of guessing.
  4. The fuse. ChatGPT never copies the edges exactly. The page lines its picture up, matches the colour, and cuts the seam where the old and new pictures agree most — round a tree rather than through it.
  5. Order. Paint outward from the town: the gold-outlined squares are ready because their neighbours are finished.
