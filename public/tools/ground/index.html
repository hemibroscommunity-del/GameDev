    How it works

    1. Make the style key first, in the World Builder. Every swatch is matched to it.
    2. For each swatch below: copy its prompt into a new ChatGPT chat, and attach the style key, and only the key. Not your bro: the world is finer, more detailed pixel art than he is, and ChatGPT copies what it sees.
    3. Bring the picture back as version A. The page makes it seamless (by cutting where its two ends already look alike, so nothing in it is see-through), keeps it at your phone's own sharpness, puts it on the shared colours, and shows it laid on the map at game size next to your bro.
    4. Later, a second version (B) of the same prompt in a fresh chat. The game mixes A and B so the ground does not repeat.
    5. Edge pieces (optional, under each swatch): in the same chat, ask for that ground's loose pieces on magenta. The game scatters them where it lies over another ground.
    6. Blends (optional, below the swatches): where two alike grounds mix, a third picture made from the two, laid through the middle. Start with the town square.
    7. Walk on them in the game whenever you like (below): it lays them straight from this page, nothing to send.
    8. When you are happy, Download all and upload the zip to GitHub (below), so every player gets them. A session puts it in the game.

    Start with the commons, a road and the first stage of one spoke: that is enough to judge the look round town. Pictures stay in this browser; nothing is uploaded.

    Where two grounds meet

    The game draws every edge between two grounds itself, from the two swatches:

    • The higher ground lies over the lower. From the bottom up: lava; roads and the railway bed; bare rock; metal floor plates; earth and mud; sand and ash; moss; grass; ice; snow. So grass reaches over dirt, sand drifts onto rock, and snow lies on everything.
    • Each pair has its own edge. A road's edge is narrow; sand drifting onto rock is wide. Inside it, the two pictures interlock along their own tufts and lumps, so the edge looks drawn, not cut.
    • A land's stages change over a wide area, in patches, not along a line.
    • Grounds that are much alike mix over a wide zone, in big patches, instead of meeting at an edge: the town square into the yards and the street, one meadow into the next, sand into dry earth (v2.3.2950). Through a blend picture, if you make one (below).
    • The boardwalks and bridges keep straight edges, and the water its shore.

    Edge pieces (optional). Every ground that lies over others has one more prompt, under its swatch: its own loose tufts, lumps or drifts on a plain magenta background. The game scatters them along that ground's edges.

    Why one per ground and not one per pair: about 200 pairs of grounds touch on the Wheel (the road alone meets 45), and one set of pieces works against every neighbour. Make a ground's edge pieces in the same chat as its swatch.

    The swatches

    Blends between alike grounds optional

    Where two alike grounds meet, the game mixes them over a wide strip. A blend is a third picture, made in ChatGPT from the two, that the game lays through the middle of that strip, so one ground turns into the other.

    1. Make both grounds first.
    2. Copy the pair's blend prompt into a new ChatGPT chat, and attach the two ground pictures (the button beside the prompt saves them to your phone). Not the style key: the two pictures already carry the style.
    3. Bring the picture back here. The preview jumps to where the two meet, with the blend laid.

    Each one is optional: a pair without one keeps the plain mix. The phone hardly notices (the ground takes about as long to lay, and a blend holds 1 MB only while you are near its pair). Start with the town square.

    The shared colours automatic

    Every swatch is moved onto one palette of 128 colours, made from the style key and the swatches so far (the water, night and light colours the game draws are always kept). Freeze it once you are happy with the first few: after that, every new swatch is moved onto exactly these colours.

    Save and send

    Download all is your backup: one big zip with every picture as ChatGPT made it, every swatch as the game will use it, and the colours. Keep it in iCloud or Google Drive, not on GitHub. Restore brings everything back on any device.

    Download for the game is what goes on GitHub: only the finished swatches, saved small (same pixels, about half the size), split into zips of under 25 MB so GitHub's website takes each one.

    Putting the swatches in the game
    1. Tap Download for the game, then tap each Save part button that appears.
    2. On GitHub, open the repository, tap Add file → Upload files, choose every part, and commit them (any folder is fine, for example art).
    3. Tell Claude the swatches are uploaded. A session unpacks them into the game and checks them.
