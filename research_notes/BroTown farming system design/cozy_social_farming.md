## 1. Stardew Valley: tilling, watering and sprinklers, fertilizer, quality, seasons, regrowing crops, buffs, co-op, and what made the loop work

### Takeaway
The daily loop is till, plant, water, then harvest. It stays satisfying because a steady upgrade ladder turns the dullest step, watering, into a one-time investment. Tools reach 1, 3, 5, 9 and then 18 tiles. Sprinklers water 8, 24 or 48 tiles. Retaining soil keeps a tile watered 33%, 66% or 100% of nights. Fertilizer plus farming level push crops up a visible price ladder: x1, x1.25 (silver), x1.5 (gold), x2 (iridium). The harshest rule is crop death at the change of season, which is a calendar rule. An unwatered crop does not die; it just doesn't grow that day. Co-op is one shared farm owned by a host. Farmhands cannot play while the host is offline. Money can be pooled or split.

### Cited Findings
**Tilling and tool reach (the "dig" and "water" steps)**
- Hoe reach by upgrade: basic 1 tile; copper 3 tiles in a row; steel 5 in a row; gold 3x3; iridium 3x6. Each upgraded reach is used by holding the button to charge. — [TheGamer tool guide](https://www.thegamer.com/stardew-valley-how-to-upgrade-and-use-every-tool/); [Assorted Meeples](https://assortedmeeples.com/stardew-valley-tool-upgrade-guide) (via search)
- Watering can by upgrade: basic 1 tile, 40 charges; copper 3-tile line, 55; steel 5-tile line, 70; gold 3x3, 85; iridium 6x3, 100. Upgrade costs, the same for both tools: copper 5 copper bars + 2,000g; steel 5 iron bars + 5,000g; gold 5 gold bars + 10,000g; iridium 5 iridium bars + 25,000g. — [TheGamer tool guide](https://www.thegamer.com/stardew-valley-how-to-upgrade-and-use-every-tool/); [Assorted Meeples](https://assortedmeeples.com/stardew-valley-tool-upgrade-guide) (via search)

**Watering automation (sprinklers)**
- Quality Sprinkler waters 8 tiles. Iridium Sprinkler waters 24 tiles (5x5). A Pressure Nozzle adds one tile of range in every direction: basic becomes 3x3, quality becomes 5x5 (24 tiles), and iridium becomes 7x7 (48 tiles). — [TheGamer sprinklers guide](https://www.thegamer.com/stardew-valley-sprinklers-guide-enricher-pressure-nozzle/); [stardewvalleyids.com](https://stardewvalleyids.com/sprinklers) (via search)
- An unwatered crop does not die, but it doesn't grow that day. — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops) (via search)

**Fertilizer and soil**
- Speed-Gro makes crops grow 10% faster, Deluxe Speed-Gro 25% and Hyper Speed-Gro 33%. Basic Retaining Soil has about a 33% chance of keeping a tile watered overnight, Quality about 66% and Deluxe 100%. With Deluxe, one watering lasts until harvest. — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Fertilizer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Fertilizer); [Deluxe Retaining Soil](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Deluxe_Retaining_Soil); [GameRant fertilizer guide](https://gamerant.com/stardew-valley-fertilizer-guide/) (via search)
- Quality roll: goldChance = 0.2 × (L/10) + 0.2 × F × ((L+2)/12) + 0.01. L is the farming level. F is the fertilizer level: 0 none, 1 Basic, 2 Quality, 3 Deluxe. Iridium is rolled at goldChance/2, and only with Deluxe Fertilizer (F = 3), the only way to get iridium crops. Deluxe Fertilizer at farming level 0 gives 84% silver, 10% gold and 6% iridium. I re-derived these numbers from the formula (goldChance 0.11, iridium 0.055, gold about 0.104, silver the remaining 0.841), so they are consistent. The 84% silver at level 0 means Deluxe never yields a normal-quality crop. — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Deluxe Fertilizer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Deluxe_Fertilizer); [Stardew Valley Wiki: Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops) (via search)

These imply silver ×1.25, gold ×1.5 and iridium ×2, rounded down. — [Wiki: Strawberry](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Strawberry); [Wiki: Cranberries](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Cranberries); [TheGamer crop price guide](https://www.thegamer.com/stardew-valley-complete-crop-sell-price-guide/); [hardcoregamer crop quality guide](https://hardcoregamer.com/stardew-valley/crop-quality-guide/) (via search)

**Seasons and crop death**
- When the season changes, after day 28, crops wither and die. The Greenhouse and the Ginger Island farm ignore seasons: any crop grows there at any time. Giant crops do not die at the change of season. — [Wiki: Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops); [Wiki: Ginger Island](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Ginger_Island); [GameRant Ginger Island guide](https://gamerant.com/stardew-valley-ginger-island-farming-guide/) (via search). The search summary also concluded that unwatered crops "survive seasonal transitions". That was the summarizer's own inference, and it almost certainly misreads the wiki (an unwatered crop still dies at the season change), so I discard it.

**Food and drink buffs**
- One food buff and one drink buff can be active at the same time. Eating a new food replaces the old food buff without touching the drink buff, and a new drink replaces only the old drink buff. Luck, Speed and Max Energy come from both foods and drinks, so those three stack. Durations are given in real-time minutes and seconds, the same for every effect of one item (Squid Ink Ravioli is the exception). Qi Seasoning makes a buff last 50% longer. — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Buffs](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Buffs); [GameRant buffs guide](https://gamerant.com/stardew-valley-every-buff-and-how-to-get-them/) (via search)

**Co-op (farmhands, money, permissions)**
- The player who created the world is the host; the others are "farmhands". A farmhand can join only if an empty cabin is free, and each cabin belongs to one player. Robin builds a cabin for 100g, 100 Wood and 5 Stone. — [Wiki: Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer); [PC Gamer co-op guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-co-op-multiplayer-guide/); [gamers.wiki multiplayer guide](https://gamers.wiki/en/games/stardew-valley/guides/stardew-valley-multiplayer-guide-how-to-play-with-friends) (via search)
- Money is either shared or kept in separate wallets. It is chosen when the farm is created and can be changed later at the Town Ledger in the Mayor's Manor. PC Gamer's framing: separate money is "a good choice if you have a friend who won't stop buying seeds when you were trying to save for that backpack upgrade." — [PC Gamer co-op guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-co-op-multiplayer-guide/); [Wiki: Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer) (via search)
- Host-only controls: the chat commands /kick, /ban, /unban, /unbanAll, /pause, /resume, /moveBuildingPermission off|owned|on and /unlinkPlayer. "Building Move Mode" decides who may ask Robin to move buildings: everyone, the host only, or each building's owner only. — [Wiki: Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer); [GamesRadar co-op guide](https://www.gamesradar.com/stardew-valley-co-op-multiplayer-how-to/) (via search)
- Version 1.6 raised co-op to up to 8 players on PC: the host plus 7 farmhands. — [GamingBolt 1.6 news](https://gamingbolt.com/stardew-valley-update-1-6-out-now-includes-8-player-multiplayer-new-festival-and-more); [GameServerKings](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/stardew-valley/stardew-valley-multiplayer/) (via search)
- The farm exists only while the host is playing. With the host offline, farmhands cannot reach the world or their characters. The workarounds are hosted servers or the "Always On Server" mod. — [GameServerKings](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/stardew-valley/stardew-valley-multiplayer/); [Wiki: Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer) (via search)

**What the designer and players said made the loop work**
- Eric Barone wanted "a really relaxed and joyous experience" that wasn't stressful and didn't make players feel bad. He said Grandpa's evaluation "breaks the relaxed feel of the game" and made it more open-ended. He wanted many ways to progress, so a player can skip farming entirely for the mines or fishing. He contrasted the game's "relaxed pace" with overstimulating games, saying players report feeling "invigorated after playing Stardew Valley as opposed to being burnt out." — [Kotaku: Past, Present and Future of Stardew Valley](https://kotaku.com/the-past-present-and-future-of-stardew-valley-1766238624); [NPR, 2025-01-24](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/24/g-s1-44510/the-legacy-and-future-of-the-farming-game-stardew-valley); [EGW 10-year interview](https://egw.news/gaming/news/26145/stardew-valley-is-10-years-developer-interview-abo-RE7ooIF8I); [Cheerful Ghost interview](https://cheerfulghost.com/jdodson/posts/1005/interview-w-eric-barone-creator-of-stardew-valley) (via search; I cannot tell which quote came from which interview)
- Player sentiment on watering: one player hand-watered for the whole first year and was "very close to quitting". At about 50 plants, watering uses the whole energy bar and half the day. Quality Sprinklers are called among the most useful things in the game ("if you have enough of them, you won't have to water anything at all"). Basic sprinklers are seen as weak unless built in large numbers. — Steam discussions: [1](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/3415432674407977809), [2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/1694914736006993953), [3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/142261027577094173) (via search; which thread said what is uncertain)

### Inferences
- The best-proven cure for farming tedium is an automation ladder bought with progress. Each early chore is collapsed in turn by a tool upgrade (1 → 3 → 5 → 9 → 18 tiles), placed automation (sprinklers: 8/24/48 tiles) or a soil consumable (33/66/100% retention). For BroTown's dig/plant/fertilize/water steps, give each step an upgrade path that eventually batches or removes it. The steps then stay satisfying at 9 plots and still work at 90.
- Fertilizer feels worth it because its payoff is a visible, legible tier with a fixed price multiplier (×1.25 / ×1.5 / ×2) and a guaranteed floor (Deluxe never gives normal quality). A BroTown fertilize step should change a number the player can see at harvest.
- Stardew keeps its punishments on the calendar (season change), never on absence: missing water only pauses a crop. On a real-time server where days pass while the player is offline, a season-style wipe would punish absence. That would break the "no punishment" feel that Barone prized.
- The one-food-plus-one-drink buff rule is a simple, readable cap that makes cooked crops matter in combat without stacking abuse. It suits an ARPG.
- Stardew's co-op weakness is that it depends on the host. A server-hosted farm has no such limit, so friends can help asynchronously. None of the four games fully offers that safely (see sections 2 and 4).

## 2. Palia: garden plots and expansion, crop needs, garden buffs, star quality, helping friends, crops feeding cooking/buffs/gold, sentiment

### Takeaway
Palia is the closest analogue to a server-based, real-time farm. One in-game day is one real hour, so a 3-12-day crop takes about 3-12 real hours. Crops need one watering a day and never die if neglected: they pause, and weeds also pause them. "Garden buffs" from orthogonal neighbours make layout a puzzle, and the same buffs remove chores (Water Retain, Weed Block). Plot expansion costs Renown, a currency earned by playing, at a rising price. Friends can water and weed your crops only while you are online and standing on your plot. Players called hand-watering tedious and asked for sprinklers and shared homes.

### Cited Findings
**Time and grow times**
- One in-game day takes one real hour, and each in-game hour is 2.5 minutes. Crop growth is counted in in-game days. — [Palia Wiki: Time Passage guide](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Guide:Time_Passage_in_Palia); [Palia Fandom: Time](https://palia.fandom.com/wiki/Time) (via search)
- Grow times in in-game days (each about one real hour): Carrot 3, Rice 3, Tomato 4, Onion 4, Wheat 4, Cotton 4, Potato 5 (about 5 real hours), Blueberry 9, Apple 12. — [Prima Games: How long do crops take to grow in Palia](https://primagames.com/tips/how-long-do-crops-take-to-grow-in-palia-answered); [Palia Wiki: Time Passage guide](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Guide:Time_Passage_in_Palia) (via search)

**Crop needs (water, weeds, fertilizer) and absence**
- A crop needs one watering a day, and its water level resets at 6 am in-game. If the water bar isn't full, the plant doesn't grow. An unwatered crop pauses but never dies. — [TheGamer Palia gardening guide](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-farming-gardening-seeds-soil-plots-fertilizer-guide-walkthrough/); [Steam: "Do gardens still tick if don't return to my plot?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707930/discussions/0/4701286722076382830/) (via search)
- Each day a plant has a small chance to grow a weed, unless it has WeedBlock Fertilizer or the weed-prevention crop buff. A weeded plant stops growing and cannot be watered. — [TheGamer Palia gardening guide](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-farming-gardening-seeds-soil-plots-fertilizer-guide-walkthrough/); [Palia Wiki: Gardening](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Gardening) (via search)
- There are five fertilizers: QualityUp, HydroPro (the crop needs no water), SpeedyGro, WeedBlock and HarvestBoost. — [TheGamer Palia gardening guide](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-farming-gardening-seeds-soil-plots-fertilizer-guide-walkthrough/) (via search)

**Garden buffs (adjacency / crop pairing)**
- Water Retain: Potato, Napa Cabbage, Tomato. Neighbours stay watered with no daily watering. Weed Block: Carrot, Onion, Bok Choy. Stops knapweed growing on neighbours. Harvest Boost: Wheat, Rice and Corn (1x1), Blueberry and Batterfly Beans (2x2), Apple (3x3). Gives +50% extra crop yield. Quality Boost: Cotton. Growth Speed: the same summary also lists Blueberries, Apples and Spicy Peppers here, which contradicts its own Harvest Boost list for Blueberry and Apple. Unresolved; treat these two crops' buffs as unverified. — [Palia Wiki: Gardening](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Gardening); [TheGamer best farm layouts](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-best-farm-layouts/); [paliaguide.com crops](https://paliaguide.com/crops); [MetaForge advanced gardening](https://metaforge.app/palia/advanced-gardening-guide-to-palia) (via search)
- Rules: a buff reaches only orthogonal neighbours, never diagonals. A crop never buffs its own type (a Tomato's Water Retain doesn't reach other tomatoes). The multi-tile crops (Blueberries, Spicy Peppers, Batterfly Beans, Rockhopper Pumpkins, Apples) need several adjacent crops of the same buff type to receive a buff, on any sides. Bonuses are applied at 6 am each day, not at harvest. — [Palia Wiki: Gardening](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Gardening); [TheGamer best farm layouts](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-best-farm-layouts/); [Palia Wiki: Star Quality](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Star_Quality) (via search)
- The layout puzzle supports a third-party tool ecosystem (garden planners, layout guides). — [Palia Garden Planner](https://paliagardenplanner.com/); [Dot Esports: 10 best garden layouts](https://dotesports.com/general/news/10-best-garden-layouts-in-palia); [GameRant best farm layouts](https://gamerant.com/palia-best-farm-layouts/) (via search)

**Star quality, cooking, Focus (how crops feed the rest of the game)**
- A dish cooked with star-quality ingredients gives more Focus or sells for more gold. Star-quality dishes give 1.5x the base Focus. Focus adds a percentage bonus to skill XP (shown as EXP + Focus Bonus%). The bonus depends on how full the Focus bar is, not on the food's quality. Dishes with more ingredients give more cooking XP, and star-quality food gives bonus XP. — [Palia Wiki: Star Quality](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Star_Quality); [Palia Wiki: Experience](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Experience); [GameSkinny: Focus](https://www.gameskinny.com/tips/palia-how-to-get-focus/); [newwebplay Focus guide](https://newwebplay.com/guides/palia-focus-guide/) (via search)

**Plot ownership and expansion**
- Each player has a private, instanced housing plot. To expand it you need Writs, bought from the City Hall Store with Renown: 20 Renown for the first, rising to a cap of 100 Renown per Writ. One expansion costs one Writ, there are 39 expansions, and all of them cost 2,410 Renown in total. — [Palia Wiki: Housing Expansion](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Expansion); [guided.news expansion guide](https://guided.news/en/guides/palia-how-to-expand-your-house-and-property/) (via search)
- Separately, extra "Housing Slots" (saved alternate plot designs, not more land) cost 50,000 gold, rising 25,000 each time, up to 225,000. An earlier search summary called these "plot expansion"; that was wrong. — [Palia Wiki: Housing Slots](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Slots) (via search)
- LOW CONFIDENCE (aggregator site): garden capacity of about 9 plots on the starter plot, about 18 at Expanded I, about 36 at Expanded II and 60+ at Expanded III. — [farminggames.help housing](https://farminggames.help/palia/housing/); [paliaguide.com plot guide](https://paliaguide.com/plot-guide) (via search)

**Visiting and helping on friends' plots**
- You visit through the "Visit Other Plots Board" beside your plot's entrance gate. The friend or party member must be on their own plot, and the plot must be public. Visitors can pull weeds, water crops and add materials to pending structures. An "editor" can at most move items already placed. Nobody can help while the owner is offline. — [Palia support: Social Features](https://support.palia.com/hc/en-us/articles/7475337549076-Social-Features); [Palia Wiki: Housing Plot](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Plot); [TechRaptor gardening guide](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/palia-gardening-guide); [Steam: "some questions"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707930/discussions/0/4297070044558032007/) (via search)
- Demand for deeper shared ownership, judged from thread titles only (contents not read): "Shared homes are a must !!!", "An Idea - Neighbours", "Can I build a house with my gf?". — [Steam 1](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707930/discussions/0/4360121202642738499/); [Steam 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707930/discussions/0/4631483212915301913/); [Steam 3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707930/discussions/0/4297070044555416693/) (via search)

### Inferences
- Palia's one-hour day shows the grow-time scale a real-time server farm needs. A 3-5-day crop is ready in a few real hours, so it pays off within the same day across several short sessions. Long crops (9-12 hours) make an overnight check-in natural.
- Pausing instead of dying, with weeds as the only setback (and a weed only pauses the crop), gives a light reason to check in without ever punishing absence. It fits a phone game with irregular sessions.
- Adjacency buffs are the most interesting anti-tedium idea here. The same mechanic that rewards planning (+50% yield, quality) also removes chores (Water Retain, Weed Block), so automation is earned by thinking, not by grinding. The buffs apply at a fixed daily tick (6 am), which keeps them server-friendly and easy to read.
- Two warnings from sentiment. Don't make watering a hold-for-seconds action, and make tool reach a predictable shape (Stardew's lines and rectangles), never random.
- Palia's rule that the owner must be present makes helping a friend a scheduling problem. With short, out-of-sync phone sessions, that means friends rarely meet. Visits to an offline friend's farm, limited to beneficial actions, would be the improvement (see section 5).
- Paying for expansions in a play-earned currency at a rising price (20 → 100 Renown) is the "free starter plot, paid expansions" model without real-money land. It also spreads the 39 expansions across a long progression.

## 3. ArcheAge: land ownership, plot scarcity and taxes, crops on public land and theft, lessons from the land-grab and bot problems

### Takeaway
ArcheAge put farms in one shared, non-instanced world. Land was finite and held by paying taxes, with tax certificates bought with labor, and by holding Patron (subscription) status. Any plot whose taxes lapsed could be demolished and claimed by anyone after a short countdown. The results were land rushes, scripts that auto-sniped expiring plots, alts used as "tax mules", land barons holding 30-50 plots, and a claimed quarter of players with no land at all. Crops planted on open land could be stolen, at the price of crime points and a player-jury trial. It is the cautionary tale: scarcity plus theft breeds griefing, bots and real-money pressure. The NA/EU service closed in 2024.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Shared, finite land turns farming into a race. The winners are the people with scripts and alts, and the losers are casual players, who are BroTown's phone audience. A private, instanced farm per player, as the owner wants, removes the whole category of problem: nothing to snipe, nothing to squat.
- ArcheAge needed a paid gate to stop land squatting with throwaway accounts. If a BroTown farm is private and tied to the persistent identity, a free starter plot can be squatted by no one. Expansions are then pure progression, not a scarce resource, whatever currency they cost.
- Taxes that lapse into demolition are the harshest answer to "what if I'm away": a week away could cost a player everything they built. Avoid upkeep that destroys property. If any upkeep exists, it should only pause production.
- Theft as content, with crime points and juries, made headlines but also made crops a griefing target. For visitors to a friend's farm, the lesson is to allow only actions that help the owner, never ones that take from them.

## 4. Animal Crossing: New Horizons: visiting islands, watering friends' flowers, permission tiers

### Takeaway
ACNH's social farming is light and hard to grief. A visitor can water your flowers, and each extra visitor who waters a flower raises its chance to reproduce, from 5% alone to 80% with five visitors. That makes helping meaningful, harmless and capped. Destructive tools (axe, shovel) need Best Friend status. Visit access comes in tiers: all friends, best friends only, or anyone holding a temporary Dodo code. A Dream Address lets anyone tour a frozen, read-only snapshot of the island while its owner is offline. Absence costs only cosmetic upkeep (weeds, then cockroaches after a month), and New Horizons removed flower wilting.

### Cited Findings
- Flower reproduction chance by the number of distinct visitors who watered the flower that day: 0 visitors 5%, 1 visitor 25%, 2 visitors 35%, 3 visitors 50%, 4 visitors 65%, 5 or more 80%. Up to five visitors count. Your own watering gives the flat 5%, and visitors watering with you raise the odds of offspring and so of hybrid colours. — [Nookipedia: Flower](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Flower); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/animal-crossing-new-horizons-how-breed-more-flowers-friends); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/how-to-crossbreed-flowers-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/); [Bell Tree Forums: "have your friends water your flowers"](https://www.belltreeforums.com/threads/want-more-hybrids-have-your-friends-water-your-flowers.517292/) (via two separate searches, which agree)
- In New Horizons, flowers no longer wilt by default; watering matters only for breeding. In New Leaf, wilted flowers turned brown and were destroyed if picked, but could be saved by watering them the same day. — [Animal Crossing Fandom: Flowers](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Flowers); [Nookipedia: Watering can](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Watering_can); [GameFAQs: "Do flowers wilt if you dont water them?"](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/248082-animal-crossing-new-horizons/78517430) (via search)
- Best Friends: added through the Best Friends app on the NookPhone, and only someone who has already visited can be added. Only Best Friends may use potentially destructive tools such as axes and shovels on your island; other visitors "cannot use these tools under any circumstances". — [Nintendo UK: tips for playing with friends (May 2020)](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html); [Digital Trends: adding friends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/how-to-add-friends-animal-crossing-new-horizons/) (via search)

### Inferences
- The watering bonus that grows with each visitor is the strongest "why visit a friend's farm" pattern for BroTown. Each distinct helper raises the odds of a good outcome for the owner (a rare crop, a quality tier, a seed). The bonus is capped (five helpers), takes nothing from the owner, needs no trust, and rewards visiting many friends briefly, which suits short phone sessions. It must count distinct helpers per crop per day, or one friend could spam it.
- ACNH's permission tiers map directly onto a farm. Any visitor gets beneficial actions only (water, pull weeds). Trusted "best friends" get actions that change the land (dig, plant, place). Only the owner harvests and sells.
- A Dream-style read-only snapshot is a cheap way to show off a farm to anyone, any time, with no griefing surface. On a server, the live farm could be shown read-only, so no separate snapshot is needed.
- Absence costs are cosmetic, accumulate slowly, and are fixed in seconds (weeds, cockroaches after a month). Nothing the player built is ever lost. This is the ACNH answer to "what if I'm away".

- Not found: weed regrowth rates and the size of the island-rating effects.

## 5. Cross-cutting: what each game does about absence, why visit a friend's farm, and which patterns suit a phone-first multiplayer game with short sessions

### Takeaway
Three of the four games keep absence harmless for crops. Palia pauses them, ACNH removed wilting, and Stardew lets an unwatered crop sit (its seasonal death is a calendar rule). ArcheAge alone made absence destructive: lapsed taxes meant demolition. The four reasons to visit a friend's farm are, in rising order of griefing risk: helping (ACNH's visitor bonus; Palia's water and weed), sharing one farm (Stardew co-op), showing off (ACNH dreams), and taking (ArcheAge theft). A phone-first, server-based game can combine the safe ones and go further than any of the four by letting friends help asynchronously while the owner is offline.

### Cited Findings
**What happens when a player is away**
- Stardew: an unwatered crop doesn't grow but doesn't die; crops die at the season change; the Greenhouse and Ginger Island ignore seasons. — [Wiki: Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops); [Wiki: Ginger Island](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Ginger_Island) (via search). In co-op, the farm exists only while the host plays. — [GameServerKings](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/stardew-valley/stardew-valley-multiplayer/) (via search)
- Palia: time runs in real time (1 day = 1 hour). Unwatered crops pause and never die; weeds pause growth. — [Palia Wiki: Time Passage guide](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Guide:Time_Passage_in_Palia); [TheGamer Palia gardening guide](https://www.thegamer.com/palia-farming-gardening-seeds-soil-plots-fertilizer-guide-walkthrough/) (via search)
- ArcheAge: taxes must be paid in a 2-week window. After that the structure is unprotected and anyone can demolish it and claim the land. Patron status is also needed to pay. — [saarith](https://saarith.com/archeage-how-to-get-a-house/); [Ten Ton Hammer](https://www.tentonhammer.com/guides/archeage-housing-guide-construction-and-upkeep) (via search)
- ACNH: flowers don't wilt. Weeds spread, and cockroaches come only after more than a month away. — [Animal Crossing Fandom: Flowers](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Flowers); [Nookipedia: Cockroach](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Cockroach) (via search)

**Why visit, and the anti-griefing guardrails**
- Stardew: one shared farm; a wallet choice (shared or separate); the host controls kicks, bans and building moves. — [Wiki: Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-co-op-multiplayer-guide/) (via search)
- Palia: visitors water, weed and add materials, but only while the owner is present and the plot is public; an editor can only move placed items. — [Palia support: Social Features](https://support.palia.com/hc/en-us/articles/7475337549076-Social-Features) (via search)
- ArcheAge: crops on open land can be stolen, deterred only by crime points and a jury; private farms are limited to the owner and people they permit. — [ArcheAge Fandom: Justice System](https://archeage.fandom.com/wiki/Justice_System); [saarith](https://saarith.com/archeage-how-to-start-farming-get-scarecrow-farm/) (via search)
- ACNH: a reproduction bonus that grows with each visitor (5% to 80%); destructive tools limited to Best Friends; read-only dream visits while the owner is offline. — [Nookipedia: Flower](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Flower); [Nintendo UK tips](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html); [Nintendo Support: Dream Island](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-(animal-crossing:-new-horizons)) (via search)

**Ownership and expansion models**
- Stardew: one farm per save. In co-op, cabins are per player (100g + 100 Wood + 5 Stone each). — [gamers.wiki](https://gamers.wiki/en/games/stardew-valley/guides/stardew-valley-multiplayer-guide-how-to-play-with-friends) (via search)
- Palia: a private instanced plot, expanded 39 times with Writs bought for 20 to 100 Renown each, 2,410 Renown in total. Alternate design slots cost 50,000+ gold. — [Palia Wiki: Housing Expansion](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Expansion); [Palia Wiki: Housing Slots](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Slots) (via search)
- ArcheAge: finite shared land, gated by Patron status and held by taxes. — [Engadget 2014](https://www.engadget.com/2014/04/03/archeage-faq-mentions-testing-plans-optional-subscription-and/) (via search)

### Inferences
These are my synthesis for BroTown's stated goals: dig, plant, fertilize and water steps; per-crop grow times; a private farm friends can be invited to; a free starter plot with paid expansions.
- **Absence: grow on server time and never kill a crop for neglect.** Count grow time in real minutes and hours on the server, as Palia does with its one-hour day. Short crops then finish within a session and long ones overnight. An unwatered crop should pause (Palia, Stardew), and a ripe crop should wait in the ground. Keep seasonal or other wipe rules out of a game whose clock runs while the player is offline. Any negative event should be cosmetic or pause-only, like Palia's weeds or ACNH's weeds and cockroaches.
- **Tedium: a ladder that automates each step.** Use Stardew's three parallel ladders. Tool reach goes 1 → 3 → 5 → 9 → 18 tiles. Placed automation is the sprinkler (8/24/48 tiles). Consumables are retaining soil (33/66/100%) and Palia's HydroPro and WeedBlock. Palia's adjacency buffs are a fourth, puzzle-based ladder. Avoid what Palia players disliked: hold-to-water timers and unpredictable tool reach.
- **Satisfaction: make each step show at harvest.** A quality tier with a fixed price multiplier (Stardew's ×1.25/×1.5/×2; Palia's star quality at 1.5x Focus) and a yield bonus (Palia's +50% Harvest Boost) make fertilizing and layout choices visible. Feeding crops into the ARPG's own loop through cooking, with a capped buff rule (Stardew's one food plus one drink; Palia's Focus as an XP bonus), gives farming a reason inside combat play. Farming should stay optional, as in Barone's "skip farming entirely".
- **Visiting: ACNH's helper bonus plus permission tiers, made asynchronous.** Let any friend visit and water or weed. Give a per-crop, per-day bonus that grows with each distinct helper, ACNH-style and capped. Reserve land-changing actions (dig, plant) for a trusted tier, and harvesting and selling for the owner. ACNH (Dream Address) and Palia (owner must be present) both refused live visits to an offline owner's farm. A server-authoritative farm could allow them, but only for helpful, capped, server-checked actions. This is new ground; none of the four games does it, so it carries the most design risk and the least precedent.
- **Showing off:** a read-only view of a friend's farm, the Dream pattern, is cheap and cannot be griefed.
- **Expansions:** a private, instanced starter plot with expansions bought at a rising price. Palia uses a currency earned by playing (Renown, 20 → 100 per Writ); Stardew uses gold plus materials. Either avoids every ArcheAge failure: land rushes, sniping scripts, alt tax mules, subscription gating, and demolition for missing taxes.

### Gaps
- None of the four games is phone-first. Stardew has mobile versions, but I found nothing on its mobile multiplayer. I found no source measuring how these absence and visit rules affect session length or retention.
- Not found: any data on how often players actually visit friends to help (Palia's visit board, ACNH watering) or on griefing incidents in Palia's helper permissions.
- Every number in these notes comes from search-result summaries, not full-page reads, because of the egress block. The report writer may want to spot-check the core tables (Stardew fertilizer and quality, Palia buffs, the ACNH visitor table) if any tool can read those wikis.
